Saskatoon homeowners often assume City Hall handles every permit. For wiring, the City itself says otherwise.

## Who issues electrical permits?

The City of Saskatoon’s [garage](https://www.saskatoon.ca/services-residents/homebuilding-renovations-improvements/project-guides/garages) and [renovation](https://www.saskatoon.ca/services-residents/homebuilding-renovations-improvements/project-guides/home-renovation-addition-or-new-construction) guides state that **electrical and gas permits are administered by the Technical Safety Authority of Saskatchewan (TSASK)**.

- TSASK electrical: [tsask.ca/permits-inspections/electrical](https://www.tsask.ca/permits-inspections/electrical/)
- Phone: **1-866-530-8599**
- Email: info@tsask.ca

City Building Standards still issues **building** permits for garages, basements, and structural work. Many jobs need both.

## When is an electrical permit required?

TSASK’s [home/farm owner electrical wiring permit page](https://www.tsask.ca/permit-inspection/home-farm-owner-electrical-wiring-permit/) says an electrical permit is required for electrical installation work in a home or farm. Examples they list include replacing switches and outlets, **garages**, ceiling fans, connecting permanently installed appliances, and wiring of hot tubs and pools (with extra licence rules).

If a contractor is hired, **licensed electrical contractors** purchase permits through TSASK’s GEIS system. You can **ask for a copy of the permit before work begins**. TSASK says that copy is how you confirm the contractor is licensed in Saskatchewan and obligated to correct issues.

## Homeowner permits have limits

TSASK publishes a home/farm owner permit path for an owner-occupant of a **detached single-family home**. It is restricted to **single-phase, non-hazardous** installations **not exceeding 200 amps or 300 volts**.

TSASK says you **need a licensed Saskatchewan electrical contractor** (homeowner permit is not eligible) when:

- The property shares a wall (apartment, duplex, townhouse, condo)
- The owner does not live there
- The property is used as a rental
- The work is **EV chargers, generators, battery storage, or renewable-energy / net-metering systems**
- The installation exceeds 200 amps or 300 volts, is polyphase, or is in a hazardous location

Installing an **electrical service**, and some other items such as hot tubs, swimming pools, or Generlink, is only eligible on a homeowner permit if the owner or an immediate family member is a **Saskatchewan Electrical Journeyperson** (Red Seal or other provincial licences alone are not accepted, per TSASK).

Read the current TSASK page before you assume you can wire it yourself. Rules here are theirs, not ours.

## Rough-in is before drywall

TSASK describes a **rough-in inspection** as an inspection of wiring **before walls are finished**. If you close a basement or garage wall first, expect to open it.

## Other Saskatchewan cities

TSASK electrical permits apply across the province. [Martensville](https://www.martensville.ca/services-for-residents/buildings-renovations-permits/) says so on its building page. Warman, Delisle, and Prince Albert still need their own **building** permits from those municipalities.
