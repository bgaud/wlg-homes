Homeowners ask how long a garage takes as if it were one number. It is a chain of steps, some owned by the City or TSASK, some owned by weather and concrete, some owned by the crew. Here is the sequence we actually work through in Saskatoon.

## 1. Zoning and size (before anyone orders lumber)

Look up the lot on the City’s Development Portal and read the [Digital Zoning Bylaw](https://www.saskatoon.ca/digitalzoningbylaw) for that district. Height, setbacks, and coverage are district-specific. The [garage project guide](https://www.saskatoon.ca/services-residents/homebuilding-renovations-improvements/project-guides/garages) is the City’s instruction sheet for this step.

Until zoning is clear, a “how many weeks?” answer is guesswork. The [planning check](/planning-check) is a way to write down lot notes and how you will use the building before the site visit.

## 2. Building permit

A garage over **10 square metres (107 square feet)** needs a [building permit](https://www.saskatoon.ca/services-residents/homebuilding-renovations-improvements/getting-started/building-permits). Applications go through [ePermitting](https://www.saskatoon.ca/epermitting).

The City of Saskatoon does **not** publish a single guaranteed review time on those pages. Do not treat a neighbour’s memory as the official clock. Track the file in ePermitting.

For comparison only: the [City of Warman](https://warman.ca/m/faq?cat=19) says a typical application can be approved in **12–16 business days**, longer if the file is complex. The [City of Prince Albert](https://www.citypa.ca/building-business-and-development/building-and-renovating/building-and-demolition-permits/) says processing **may take up to 20 days**, longer if information is missing. Those figures apply in those cities, not automatically in Saskatoon.

## 3. Locates and site work

The garage guide: call **SaskPower at 1-888-757-6937** before you dig. Tree protection applies within six metres of a City tree. A new crossing needs a traffic permit.

Skipping locates to “save a week” is how you hit a gas line. We do not skip them.

## 4. Concrete pad

Framing does not start on wet concrete. Pad pour and cure sit on the weather and the mix, not on a marketing calendar. If you already have a cured pad that matches the approved drawings, this step is already done.

## 5. Shell: framing, wrap, siding, roof

This is the part people picture. On **one completed Saskatoon double garage**, after the pad had cured, WLG Homes closed in the shell — framing through siding and shingles — in **two days** of on-site work (about 28 hours). That story is on our [garage build photo post](/blog/garage-build-saskatoon).

That is a real job, not a promise for a frost-wall foundation, an oversized shop, or a winter week with a blizzard. Larger buildings and finished interiors add days. We give you a schedule for **your** drawings at estimate time.

## 6. Electrical

[TSASK](https://www.tsask.ca/permits-inspections/electrical/) issues the electrical permit. Rough-in is inspected **before** interior finishes hide the cable. Lights and receptacles are typical; a sub-panel or 240V circuit depends on the house service. EV chargers require a licensed Saskatchewan electrical contractor under TSASK’s published rules.

## 7. Inspections and close-out

The building permit lists required inspections. Book them when that stage is actually ready. A garage that is “almost done” but missing an inspection is not done.

## What you can do to keep the file moving

- Decide size and use (vehicles only, or a heated shop) before drawings
- Send a plot plan or lot measurements with the [quote request](/contact)
- Do not start construction before the permit — Warman and Prince Albert both warn against it; Saskatoon requires the permit for the structure
- Answer City or TSASK information requests the same week they arrive
