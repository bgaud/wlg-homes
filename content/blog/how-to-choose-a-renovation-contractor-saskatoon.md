Choosing a renovation contractor in Saskatoon is a decision that affects more than just your budget. It determines whether your project gets done to code, on schedule, and by people who actually stand behind the work. Here's what to check before you sign anything.

## Why Does Choosing the Right Renovation Contractor Matter?

A renovation touches your home's structure, electrical system, and resale value. A contractor who cuts corners on framing or wiring can create problems that don't show up until years later, like a wall that isn't properly load-bearing or a circuit that's overloaded. Vetting a contractor properly upfront costs you nothing and saves you from expensive fixes down the road.

## What Should You Ask a Renovation Contractor Before Hiring?

### Are They Licensed and Insured?

Any contractor doing electrical work in Saskatchewan must have a licensed electrician on staff or subcontracted for that portion. Ask directly: "Who is doing the electrical, and are they licensed?" A legitimate contractor will answer without hesitation. General liability insurance is also non-negotiable. It protects you if something goes wrong on your property during the project.

### Do They Subcontract the Work?

This is one of the most overlooked questions. Many renovation companies sell you the job, then hand it off to subcontractors you've never met and can't vet. That's not necessarily a red flag on its own, but you deserve to know who's actually going to be in your house. WLG Homes doesn't subcontract renovation work. When you hire us, you get one of the owners, an experienced framer and licensed electricians, on your job site.

### Will They Pull the Necessary Permits?

Basement development, structural changes, and electrical work all require permits from the City of Saskatoon. A contractor who suggests skipping permits to save time or money is taking a shortcut that becomes your problem at resale or if there's ever an insurance claim. Ask specifically who applies for the permit and who coordinates the inspection.

### Can They Show You Past Work?

A contractor with real experience should be able to point to completed projects, whether that's photos, a portfolio, or a site you can visit. Vague answers or an unwillingness to share examples is worth taking seriously.

### Do They Give Written, In-Person Estimates?

A phone quote for a renovation is a guess, not an estimate. Layouts, mechanical obstructions, and finish choices all affect price, and none of that shows up without seeing the space. A contractor who's willing to walk your site and put a written scope and price in front of you is one who's confident in their pricing.

### How Do They Handle Multi-Trade Coordination?

If your renovation needs framing, electrical, and possibly roofing or structural work, ask how those trades will be scheduled. Separate contractors for each trade means you're the one managing the handoffs. A team that handles multiple trades in-house keeps the project moving without you chasing down schedules.
