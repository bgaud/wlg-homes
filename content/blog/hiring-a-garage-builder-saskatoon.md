A detached garage is one of the larger things you can add to a Saskatoon lot. The builder you hire decides whether the City file, the pad, the shell, and the wiring are one coordinated job or four arguments. These questions are specific to garages. For general renovations, see [how to choose a renovation contractor](/blog/how-to-choose-a-renovation-contractor-saskatoon).

## 1. Who applies for the building permit?

The City of Saskatoon requires a [building permit](https://www.saskatoon.ca/services-residents/homebuilding-renovations-improvements/getting-started/building-permits) for a garage over 10 square metres. Ask:

- Will you submit through [ePermitting](https://www.saskatoon.ca/epermitting)?
- Who draws the site plan, including driveway and City trees?
- Who pays the City’s [permit fee](https://www.saskatoon.ca/services-residents/homebuilding-renovations-improvements/getting-started/building-permit-fees)?

A builder who shrugs at zoning is asking you to own the rejection. Read [the garage permit guide](/blog/saskatoon-garage-building-permit) so you recognize a complete file.

## 2. Who pulls the electrical permit?

The City’s garage page sends electrical permits to **[TSASK](https://www.tsask.ca/permits-inspections/electrical/)**. Ask for a copy of the electrical permit before work starts. TSASK tells homeowners they can request that copy. EV chargers and generators are not homeowner-permit work under TSASK’s published rules.

If the “builder” plans to have a friend wire it cash, stop there.

## 3. Is the pad in the scope?

A garage quote that starts at “we’ll frame whatever pad you have” is incomplete unless you already have a pad that matches the drawings. Ask who coordinates the pour, who confirms thickness and anchoring, and what happens if the pad is out of square. [WLG Homes](/services/full-builds/saskatoon) coordinates the pad on full builds or starts on a pad you already poured.

## 4. Who is actually on site?

Ask for names, not a brand. Framing, siding, roofing, and electrical can be four companies you never met. WLG Homes is three owner-operators: Dawson Wiederhold (framer) and Riley Lajuenesse and Braedon Gaudet (Saskatchewan-licensed electricians). We do not subcontract that stack.

## 5. What does “complete” mean in writing?

A useful scope lists:

- Size and door sizes
- Roof type (trusses vs rafters) as shown on the City drawing
- Siding and roofing materials
- Electrical: number of lights and receptacles, sub-panel or not
- Who books inspections
- What is **excluded** (heat, insulation, slab finish, driveway concrete beyond the pad)

Phone numbers without a written scope are not estimates.

## 6. How do they talk about time?

Nobody honest can promise a completion week before the City has the permit and the pad has cured. We describe [the sequence](/blog/how-long-does-a-garage-build-take-saskatoon) and, for one finished double garage, the two-day shell after a cured pad. Ask for a schedule that names dependencies, not a slogan.

## 7. Insurance and licence

Ask for liability insurance and, for the electricians, Saskatchewan licensing. For work in Prince Albert, the City requires the contractor on the application to hold a valid Prince Albert business licence — confirm that if the garage is not in Saskatoon.

## 8. Can you see a finished building?

Photos of a real [Saskatoon garage build](/blog/garage-build-saskatoon) beat a stock rendering. Walk a completed job if they will show you one.

## After you hire someone

- Keep copies of the building permit, electrical permit, and approved drawings
- Do not let anyone start before the building permit is issued
- Call SaskPower locates before excavation (1-888-757-6937), as the City’s garage guide requires
