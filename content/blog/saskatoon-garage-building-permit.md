A garage in Saskatoon is both a construction project and a zoning project. The City publishes the rules; this article points you at those pages instead of restating numbers the City can change.

## Does a garage need a building permit in Saskatoon?

Yes, in the usual residential case. The City of Saskatoon [building permits page](https://www.saskatoon.ca/services-residents/homebuilding-renovations-improvements/getting-started/building-permits) lists a **garage** among common projects that require a building permit, along with other accessory buildings **greater than 10 square metres (107 square feet)** in floor area.

The same page lists an accessory building **less than 10 square metres** as a common project that does **not** require a building permit, provided it still conforms to zoning. If your structure is a typical single or double garage, you are over that threshold.

Demolishing or relocating a building over 10 square metres also requires a permit, according to the City’s [garage project guide](https://www.saskatoon.ca/services-residents/homebuilding-renovations-improvements/project-guides/garages).

## Zoning comes before drawings

The garage guide tells you to look up the property on the Development Portal, note the **zoning designation**, then read the [Digital Zoning Bylaw](https://www.saskatoon.ca/digitalzoningbylaw) for that district: permitted uses, discretionary uses, development standards, and landscaping.

Setbacks, height, and lot coverage are in that bylaw for **your** district. They are not a single city-wide number, so this article does not invent one. If you want help reading the lot, [request a free estimate](/contact) and we will walk it with you — the bylaw still governs.

## What a complete garage application includes

From the City’s garage guide, a complete application includes:

- The **Submission Details Form** from the Detached Garage & Accessory Building Project Guide
- A **site plan** showing lot and building dimensions, distances to property lines, and the proposed or current **driveway** location relative to sidewalks, boulevards, curbs, utilities, hydrants, and other street features. The drawing must identify **City-owned trees**. Driveway crossings within **3.0 metres** of a City-owned tree go to Urban Forestry review
- A drawing of **truss/rafter direction** and the location and sizes of **doors and windows**
- A **cross-section** in some cases, to show height of the garage above ground

Work within **six metres** of a City tree has separate tree-protection requirements ([saskatoon.ca/treepermits](https://www.saskatoon.ca/treepermits)). A new driveway or a disposal bin on the street is a traffic permit, not the building permit ([saskatoon.ca/trafficpermits](https://www.saskatoon.ca/trafficpermits)).

Call **SaskPower at 1-888-757-6937** before you dig, as the City’s garage page instructs, to locate utility lines and easements.

## How to apply

The City uses **[ePermitting](https://www.saskatoon.ca/epermitting)** so you can track the file from application through inspections. Building Standards: **306-975-2645**, [building.standards@saskatoon.ca](mailto:building.standards@saskatoon.ca).

[Permit fees](https://www.saskatoon.ca/services-residents/homebuilding-renovations-improvements/getting-started/building-permit-fees) are published by the City (including a 2026 per-square-foot rate for attached or detached garages and a minimum building permit fee). Use that page for current amounts.

## Electrical and gas are not City building permits

The garage guide states that **electrical and gas permits are administered by the Technical Safety Authority of Saskatchewan (TSASK)**. See [TSASK electrical](https://www.tsask.ca/permits-inspections/electrical/) or call **1-866-530-8599**. Wiring a garage is electrical installation work.
